# Literature Synthesis Schema

Use this report structure for dry-lab enzyme-design evidence synthesis.

## 1. Enzyme Profile And Design Anchor

Record identity, sequence scope, reaction, substrate, cofactor, objective, and
database IDs.

## 2. Function Boundary

Summarize supported functions, substrate boundaries, side reactions, and
application-relevant limits.

## 3. Mechanism And Structure

Record catalytic motifs, active-site geometry, useful structures, templates,
ligand poses, domains, interfaces, loops, and dynamics evidence.

## 4. Designable Space And Masks

Classify sites and regions as:

- `immutable`
- `restricted`
- `designable`
- `unknown`

Attach rationale, evidence IDs, and confidence.

## 5. Mutation Evidence Dataset

Separate positive variants, negative variants, contextual effects, and
transferability limits.

## 6. Homolog And Scaffold Priors

Record homolog families, structural neighbors, sequence diversity, ancestral
or consensus priors, and scaffold alternatives.

## 7. AI Design Priors

Identify relevant PLM, MPNN, inverse-folding, structure-prediction, docking,
Rosetta, MD, scaffold-search, and generative-backbone routes. Explain which
design question each route can answer.

## 8. Dry-Lab Metric Priors

Record useful computational metrics, reference baselines, calibration notes,
hard-constraint candidates, ranking priors, and uncertainty sources.

## 9. Design Opportunities

List evidence-backed opportunities and model-exploration opportunities
separately. Label provenance and risk.

## 10. Conflicts And Gap Analysis

Preserve unresolved evidence, missing inputs, and questions for computational
exploration or user decision.
